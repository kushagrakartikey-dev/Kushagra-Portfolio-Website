Business strategy

Four ways I approach an operations problem. Each pairs the general principle with what I have done, and marks recommendations and projections as such.

Cost strategy and sourcing

Start from the cost structure: what each category costs, who supplies it, how concentrated it is and what it costs to own, not only to buy. Then negotiate or reallocate where the analysis shows leverage.

In my work Rebuilt a coffee cooperative’s COGS model; the cost-allocation changes raised gross margin from 21.2% to 40.5%. Negotiated a 15% vendor-cost reduction with 12+ hotel and transport vendors.

Working-capital improvement

Cash tied up in receivables and stock is cash the business cannot use. A shorter Days Sales Outstanding and leaner, better-placed inventory release that cash and reduce how much the operation has to fund.

In my work An order-to-cash model projecting DSO from 70 to 30 days and about USD 35K released. At Decathlon, 95%+ stock availability while clearing discounted stock before new intake.

Traceability and compliance

Agri-commodities such as coffee now need plot-level traceability to reach EU buyers under the EUDR, and buyers expect human rights and environmental due diligence across the supply base. Export readiness depends on that evidence as much as on documents and Incoterms.

In my work Proposed an EUDR/HREDD traceability framework with farm-level geolocation SOPs across 1,500 supply nodes, and advised on Incoterms and export documentation for direct EU access.

Volume-gated investment decisions

Commit capital in stages tied to confirmed volume, utilisation and price thresholds. Spending then follows demand that has actually arrived, which limits exposure if it does not.

In my work Built a volume-gated capex case of about USD 85K for in-house processing to capture a $0.30/lb origin premium, and a capacity roadmap from 3 to 6 washing stations toward 20 containers a year.
